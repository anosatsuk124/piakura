# Transfer a sung phrase to piano

Use when a sung phrase becomes fragmented, a register change distorts its
contour, or a proposed melodic replacement changes its character. Start from
the supported vocal attacks, pitch movement and phrase releases. Apply the
[phrase-design guidance](phrase-design.md) to its holds, ornaments and accompaniment.

## Resolve the local ambiguity

Compare the disputed event with the whole phrase and repeated occurrences.
Distinguish a sung attack from vibrato, a scoop, a model dropout or another
instrument. Keep genuine repeated syllables and short approaches to longer notes.
Return uncertain source pitches or timing to the transcription review before
treating them as an arrangement problem.

Choose piano register over a coherent phrase, preserving its contour and high
point across vocal/instrumental handoffs. Judge harmony against the actual bass,
inner voices and resolution; the key or genre label alone does not determine
the notes of a passage.

## Preserve continuity through a necessary edit

When a local adaptation is needed under the
[retention contract](rhythm-and-articulation.md#adapt-for-listening-and-two-hand-playing),
compare the original phrase and replacement through the next arrival and release.
Set the retained note's duration as well as its gate: extending `q` within a short
allocation leaves a following rest unchanged. Respect meaningful breaths, changing
harmony and the next finger/key handoff. Use finger holding, redistribution or
clean pedal where the voice should continue beneath another attack.

Keep source ornaments and newly composed connections distinct. An added connection
needs a musical purpose, available hand space and a clear destination; a gap alone
does not require a fill. Preserve the passage's supported rhythmic and harmonic
character when choosing it.

## Review in context

Compare melody alone, accompaniment alone and both with the same piano sound,
source offset and playback gain. Include the preceding chord and its release tail.
Use the [collision guidance](arrangement.md#review-collisions-between-melody-and-accompaniment)
to distinguish supported tension from a stale or wrongly voiced chord. Record
score-based findings separately from actual listening acceptance.
