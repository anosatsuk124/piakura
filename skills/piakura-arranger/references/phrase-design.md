# Shape the piano phrase

Read before authoring a piano adaptation, including MIDI derived from singing.
Use the agreed retention contract to distinguish source correction from piano
revoicing. Keep a compact phrase plan beside the editable MML: the lead's
register and destination, bass movement, inner-voice role, accompaniment rhythm,
dynamic direction and release. Repeated phrases may share a plan; record the
musically meaningful changes at returns and climaxes.

## Protect holds and defining ornaments

Before reducing a phrase, mark its sustained structural tones, arrival/release
points and identity-bearing ornaments. A turn, grace-note approach, repeated-note
figure or short run can be essential even when it contains few milliseconds of
sound. Preserve its rhythmic placement, contour and resolution; simplify other
voices first when the hands compete. Use the least transformation that solves
the stated difficulty or register problem. A plainer skeleton is a comparison
candidate, not the default successful result.

For each structural hold, determine where the voice should continue sounding,
including underneath another voice or across an accompaniment attack. Compare
the source phrase and compiled release, and check whether finger holding,
voice redistribution or harmonically clean pedal supplies the intended duration.
A model dropout or the next accompaniment slot must not silently truncate the
lead. Equally, preserve intentional breaths and harmonic releases rather than
stretching every note. Record key-down and intended sounding continuity
separately when pedal is involved.

If an essential ornament cannot be played literally at the requested level,
retain a recognizable easier gesture and explain the change in the contract.
Check the full original gesture, proposed replacement and the following long
note together. A lossless conversion or a contract written after deletion
cannot prove that the musical information survived. Review these protected
holds and ornaments again after adding accompaniment at every difficulty.

## Register and texture

The singer's sounding octave need not be the best piano register. Compare the
lead with the preceding instrumental voice and the bass underneath it. Decide
octave changes over coherent phrases, keeping the motif, contour and supported
high point. A switch between source instruments can create an abrupt piano
register drop without being a transcription error. Declare that revoicing in
the adaptation contract instead of silently changing the source reference.

Start with melody and bass; then choose where the harmony lives. The left hand
need not carry every chord while the right hand plays only single notes. Try a
held inner voice beneath a sustained melody, a light chord at an arrival, or an
answer in a genuine gap when the hands permit it. Keep voice leading audible
and thin competing accompaniment. Easy may need only a clear tune and bass;
Hard can develop voice independence rather than merely add attacks.

Choose a rhythmic figure for its phrase function: sustained support, forward
motion, an anticipation, a response or a cadence. A chord-loop generator makes
a draft. Review its behavior at melody rests, new phrases, repeated sections,
harmonic changes and the ending. Repetition is valid when it maintains the
groove; change texture when the phrase calls for a new role or arrival.

## Dynamics, articulation and resonance

Separate melody, inner voice and bass in the actual compiled velocities. Give
the phrase a dynamic direction and an intentional arrival or retreat. Inspect
constant-role velocities and blockwise gain changes as possible omissions of
phrasing, not as automatic errors. Velocity diversity and random humanization
are not quality targets; every change should serve accent, balance or contour.

Choose releases voice by voice: a bass or inner chord tone may continue beneath
the next accompaniment attack while the melody articulates or breathes. Adapt
vocal and plucked-instrument gates to that piano phrase; their transcribed
durations are evidence, not an automatic piano articulation. Select finger
holding, redistribution or pedal according to reach and harmonic clarity.
For legato pedal changes, play the new notes, then lift and reapply the pedal
while holding them. Respect shared-channel controls, intentional rests and
releases. These are local choices, not a global
gate or pedal cycle. Compare the same piano sound with release tails, checking
both unwanted gaps and blurred harmony; key-down occupancy alone cannot describe
resonance. See [Fitch on finger holding](https://practisingthepiano.com/on-touch-part-one/)
and [Creviston on pedal changes](https://hub.yamaha.com/music-educators/instruments/piano/piano-pedagogy-pedaling/).

Review the last phrase as a piano ending. If the recording fades, decide whether
to retain a supported fade or make an explicitly arranged close within the
requested form. A file-length marker, uniform velocity ramp or trailing silence
does not by itself establish musical completion.

## Finish the musical review

Review an exposed lead, a busy phrase, a voice/texture transition, a return and
the ending. Cover other distinct textures where present. For each level, record
the actual musical decision and its evidence, including retained dissonance,
unresolved harmony or a known difficulty warning. Group genuinely equivalent
recurrences, but include their locations and inspect exceptions.

A collision flag needs a contextual disposition: supported tension and its
resolution, an intended color, a repaired pitch/timing/voicing, or an unresolved
question with a bounded location. Inspect both held notes and attacks. A shared
generic explanation for every flag does not complete that review. Do not delete
all semitone relationships or add pedal/inner notes just to satisfy a checklist.

Keep source fidelity, arrangement decisions, physical validation and listening
acceptance as separate results. Produce matched melody-only, accompaniment-only
and combined clips for disputed passages. Record score-based findings when
listening has not occurred, and label remaining musical uncertainty explicitly.
